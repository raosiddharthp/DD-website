# The Clarity Point Framework

## Definition
The Clarity Point Framework is DataDomine's proprietary approach to 
practitioner development. It is built on a single observation: every 
practitioner has experienced the moment when a complex decision resolves 
into something they can act on and defend. DataDomine is built around 
designing those moments deliberately, repeatedly, and at every level 
of the work.

The framework has ten lenses. Every DataDomine programme applies all ten.

## The Ten Lenses

### 1. The Production Ready Standard
The destination, defined first. Every programme begins by establishing 
what production-ready means for the specific system, in the specific 
context, under the specific constraints the participant will actually 
face. Not a generic definition — a negotiated, documented standard that 
the programme then works backwards from.

### 2. The Three Contexts
Production-ready has three distinct shapes:
- Greenfield: building from scratch. Rare. Requires a different kind 
  of confidence.
- Brownfield: innovating on top of existing systems. Where most careers 
  actually live.
- Integration: strapping intelligence or transformation onto a system 
  of record.
Every participant learns all three because they will encounter all three 
in their career.

### 3. Discovery Over Instruction
The Aha moment is designed in, not delivered. Every chapter is structured 
so that the participant arrives at the insight themselves. The facilitator 
designs the conditions. The participant finds the clarity. What is 
discovered is owned. What is told is forgotten.

### 4. The Jungian Behavioural Layer
Every architectural decision requires human approval before it becomes 
a built thing. The behaviour of the practitioner in the rooms where 
approval is sought determines whether technically correct decisions get 
implemented. This lens teaches the behaviours that get buy-in, pass 
the ARB, unlock the sprint, and build cross-functional trust.

### 5. Economic Decisions First
Architectural decisions are economic decisions wearing a technical coat. 
The practitioner who can present the economic argument for a design choice 
wins rooms that the practitioner who can only present the technical 
argument loses.

### 6. Agile Value Flow
Not all architectural decisions produce equal downstream value. This lens 
teaches practitioners to identify which decisions unlock flow — removing 
constraints that block multiple teams simultaneously — and how to make 
that case in sprint planning language.

### 7. The Data Foundation
All decisions, across all lenses, are informed by data. Cost data, 
constraint data, compliance data, operational data, organisational data. 
This is the foundational discipline that threads through every chapter 
of every programme.

### 8. The Failure Modes Taxonomy
Every production context has characteristic failure modes. Greenfield 
systems fail differently from brownfield systems which fail differently 
from integration systems. This lens teaches practitioners to name the 
failure modes specific to their context before they occur — and instrument 
the system to detect them early.

### 9. The Handoff Discipline
A system that cannot be handed off cleanly is not production-ready. 
This lens teaches the practitioner to give and receive handoffs with 
the rigour that a production environment requires — documenting every 
decision, verifying every assumption, and accepting ownership only of 
what has been properly transferred.

### 10. The Restraint Layer
The most undervalued capability in a technical practitioner is knowing 
when not to build. This lens teaches the discipline of recognising when 
the right answer is not a technical solution — and making that case with 
the same rigour as the argument for building.

## The Chapter Architecture
Every chapter in every DataDomine programme follows the same six-step 
structure:
1. Concept — the framework lens being applied
2. Problem Statement — the real-world problem this concept exists to solve
3. The Story — a real organisation, a real challenge, a real outcome. 
   The hero is always the solution, never the person.
4. The Problem — a specific problem presented to the participant
5. Breakout — small groups discuss possible solutions
6. Exercise with Output — a tangible artefact produced

## The Spine Project
Every cohort is built around a pre-designed spine project — a real-world 
problem presented by the facilitator in the role of the customer and 
sign-off authority. Participants work on this shared problem across all 
18 chapters. Every chapter output is a contribution to the evolving 
project artefact. The capstone is the defence of the complete artefact 
to a simulated review panel.

## Production Ready Standard
DataDomine defines production-ready across three dimensions:
- Technical: the system performs correctly under production conditions
- Organisational: the team can sustain it without the person who built it
- Compliance: every regulatory obligation is satisfied structurally, 
  not documentarily
