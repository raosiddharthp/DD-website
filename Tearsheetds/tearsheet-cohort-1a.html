The Problem

Most technical programmes teach practitioners what a system can do. Almost none teach them how to make the architectural decisions that determine whether it survives production, passes a compliance audit, or holds up under a board review.

Practitioners leave technical training knowing how to build. They do not leave knowing how to make the decision to build — or how to defend that decision under economic, regulatory, and organisational scrutiny.

The result is technically correct work that fails at the ARB, stalls in the sprint, or collapses at the first compliance review. The gap is not technical. It is architectural judgement — and it is not taught.

Who This Is For
  • Solution Architects working with enterprise AI systems
  • Enterprise Architects establishing or evolving AI design standards
  • Senior Developers moving into architecture roles in regulated environments
What You Leave With

A complete architecture artefact: ADR log, compliance mapping, economic justification, failure modes register, and handoff checklist — built across 18 chapters from a single spine project and defended before a simulated review panel at the capstone.

A portfolio piece presentable to an ARB, a compliance team, or a hiring manager. Not a certificate. An artefact.
